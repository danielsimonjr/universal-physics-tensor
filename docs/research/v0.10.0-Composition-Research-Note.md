## 1. What this is, and what it is not

This note describes an engineering experiment: a TypeScript library
("UPT") that encodes a catalog of 44 published cross-regime physics
relations ("bridge equations" — Hawking temperature, Landauer's bound,
thermal de Broglie wavelength, Shapiro delay, …) as typed, executable
graph edges, and asks whether a mechanical composition operator over
that graph can re-derive known cross-scale results — with the
acceptance criteria written down and committed *before* the
implementation existed. The author is a systems engineer, not a
physicist, and the repository says so prominently
(`docs/specification/Part-VI.md`, "Honest statement of status";
`CONTRIBUTING.md`).

It is **not** a claimed physics contribution. Every derived relation
reported in §3 is standard textbook or literature physics; nothing new
about nature is asserted. The claim, such as it is, is methodological:
that catalog-scale physics knowledge can be encoded so that (a)
composition of relations is type-checked by exact dimensional algebra,
(b) the irreducible physics judgments are isolated as small, citable,
reviewable data records rather than buried in code, and (c) the whole
exercise is disciplined by pre-registration in the version history. You
are being asked to evaluate the method and to spot-check the
judgments — §5 lists the bounded review tasks.

## 2. Method

**Typed quantity graph.** `src/composition/` defines `Quantity` (a
canonical name, an exact SI dimension vector in ℤ⁷ over {L, M, T, I, Θ,
N, J}, and sparse regime attributes) and `BridgeEdge` (n-ary sources →
one target, a numerical evaluator, a validity-domain predicate, a
confidence grade, a citation). Edges are either `bridge` (endpoint
quantities differ in at least one regime attribute) or `law`
(endpoints share regime) — a mechanical membership criterion, applied
to the whole catalog in `docs/architecture/v0.8.0-catalog-adjudication.md`.

**Composition with an exact dimensional junction check.**
`composeEdges(first, second)` (`src/composition/compose.ts`) pipes
`first`'s output into a matching source of `second`. It is defined only
if the junction quantities match **by canonical name or by an explicit
registered identification**, *and* their dimension vectors are exactly
equal (integer-vector equality — no tolerance). The interesting part is
the identification registry, `QUANTITY_IDENTIFICATIONS`: the assertion
"the Hawking temperature IS the temperature in Landauer's bound" is not
an implementation detail; it is a data record with a rationale and
citations (Hawking 1975; Bekenstein 1973), carried as provenance on
every composed edge that uses it, and explicitly offered for rebuttal.
Composed confidence is the *minimum* of the operands (established >
speculative > highly-speculative) — composition never launders
credibility. Validity domains conjoin through the pipe, so a composed
edge fails diagnosably when an operand leaves its regime.

**Pre-registration discipline.** Each composition target's formula,
acceptance tolerances, and literature anchors were committed to
`docs/planning/v0.8.0-Design.md` (§2, §9, §10) before the code. The
git ancestry is checkable (`git merge-base --is-ancestor`):

| Target | Registration commit | Implementation commit |
|---|---|---|
| CT-1 / CT-1b / CT-2 (Design §2) | `247588a` | `88b2213` |
| CT-3 (Design §9) | `4fe47f4` | `159dd5c` |
| CT-4 (Design §10) | `98595d3` | `a97e868` |

The acceptance evidence is pinned in
`tests/composition/calibration-targets.test.ts` and runs in CI on every
push. The 1e−12 relative-error pins are exact-arithmetic comparisons
(both sides built from the same committed constants), not fitted
tolerances.

## 3. Results

These realize three of the five calibration derivations (C1, C4, C5)
named in advance by the composition specification,
`docs/specification/Part-IX-Composition.md` §5 — meeting the §6
Phase-B success bar ("≥3 of C1–C5 reproduced").

**CT-1 — erasure cost at the horizon (C4).** Composing BE-42 (Hawking
temperature, M → T_H) with BE-16 (Landauer bound, T → E_min) through
the registered T_H ≡ T identification yields

    E_min(M) = ℏc³ ln2 / (8πGM)

matching the closed form to relErr ≤ 1e−12 at M ∈ {M_☉, 10 M_☉,
10⁻⁸ M_☉}; E_min(M_☉) ≈ 5.9×10⁻³¹ J. Confidence demotes to
highly-speculative — the framework's own grading of this composite.
CT-1b re-derives the identical result through a three-edge chain via
the Schwarzschild-radius *law* (M → r_s → T_H → E_min), agreeing with
the two-edge form to 1e−12. Anchors: Hawking 1975 *CMP* 43:199;
Landauer 1961 *IBM JRD* 5:183.

**CT-3 — Zurek decoherence scaling (C1).** Composing BE-12 (thermal de
Broglie wavelength, (m, T) → λ_T) with BE-11's Caldeira–Leggett
quadratic-coupling rate, junction by name (`thermal-de-broglie-wavelength`):

    Γ_dec = γ_relax · Δx² · m k_B T / (2π ℏ²)

matches to relErr ≤ 1e−12 across ≥6 decades of mass, and reproduces
Zurek's macroscopicity anchor: Γ_dec/γ_relax ∈ [10³⁹, 10⁴¹] at m = 1 g,
T = 300 K, Δx = 1 cm (the famous "~10⁴⁰"). Anchors: Zurek 1991 *Phys.
Today* 44(10):36; Zurek 2003 *RMP* 75:715; Caldeira & Leggett 1983.

**CT-2 + CT-4 — weak-field cross-observable ratios (C5).** A parallel
(shared-source) operation, `consistencyRatio`, honestly labeled as not
sequential composition: lensing/perihelion gives α/Δφ = 2a(1−e²)/(3πb),
and Shapiro/perihelion gives

    Δt/Δφ = a(1−e²)·ln(R_far/R_near) / (3πc) ≈ 1.05×10² s  (solar values)

both parameter-free in (G, M) — pinned independent of M across ≥3
decades at relErr ≤ 1e−12. Anchors: Shapiro 1964 *PRL* 13:789;
Einstein 1915.

**GW170817 confrontation — and an honest negative finding.** The
framework's first contact with real data rather than closed forms
(`src/bridges/be36-gw170817-confrontation.ts`, commit `43ddabf`, plus
its test). From the published observation record (Abbott et al. 2017
*ApJL* 848:L13 §3: Δt = 1.74 ± 0.05 s, conservative D = 26 Mpc, assumed
EM delay ≤ 10 s) it recomputes the graviton-speed bounds: upper
+c·Δt/D = +6.502×10⁻¹⁶, lower −c·(10 − 1.74)/D = −3.0865×10⁻¹⁵, whose
1-significant-figure roundings are the published +7×10⁻¹⁶ / −3×10⁻¹⁵.
The finding: UPT's catalog entry BE-36 had encoded a **symmetric**
bound |Δc/c| ≤ 10⁻¹⁵, and the published bound is asymmetric — its
negative side (−3×10⁻¹⁵) *violates* the symmetric encoding. The
confrontation result reports this rather than hiding it
(`passesEncodedBound` covers the positive side only;
`encodedBoundIsSymmetric: true` plus an explanatory note are first-class
result fields). The data pipeline caught a real defect in the catalog's
own encoding — which is the point of building one.

## 4. Limitations (read this section first if you read only one)

- **Toy scale.** The composition graph at this writing has **15 edges**
  (9 calibration, `src/composition/edges/calibration.ts`; 6
  catalog-tranche, `edges/catalog-tranche.ts`) against a 44-entry
  catalog. Three calibration derivations and one data confrontation is
  a proof of mechanism, not a survey.
- **The derived relations are known physics.** Erasure cost at T_H,
  Zurek scaling, and weak-field GR ratios are all standard. The claim
  is the *method* — pre-registered, type-checked, provenance-carrying
  re-derivation — not discovery. Whether the method produces anything a
  physicist couldn't write down in an afternoon is exactly the open
  question.
- **Single author, LLM-assisted review.** The adversarial design/plan
  reviews are performed by LLM reviewer pairs, and the repository's own
  ledgers document LLM-reviewer fabrication incidents. The governance
  document is explicit that **LLM consensus is never sufficient** to
  promote any relation's status; promotion to `established` requires a
  human-verifiable literature anchor, and data-driven claims must be
  re-runnable from the committed code (`docs/specification/Part-VI.md`
  §XXVII-B). No status was promoted by any of the work above.
- **The catalog itself is mostly speculative by its own grading**
  (8 established / 33 speculative / 3 highly-speculative at the last
  tally), and 3 membership adjudications are deliberately left open as
  contested (BE-44 soft hair, BE-46 multiverse measure, BE-50
  Wheeler–Feynman absorber). The v0.8.0 adjudication also *reversed*
  one of its own earlier classifications (BE-42, not-a-bridge → bridge)
  when the mechanical criterion was applied — the criterion is young.
- **Pre-registration here is internal**, enforced by git ancestry in a
  single-author repository — weaker than third-party registration,
  though stronger than nothing: the SHAs in §2 are immutable history on
  the public default branch.

## 5. Invitation

The repository is structured so that a physicist can contribute
judgment without reading TypeScript (`CONTRIBUTING.md`; the catalog is
published as reviewable JSON at `data/bridge-catalog.json`). The
highest-value bounded tasks, each a self-contained literature judgment:

1. **The three contested adjudications** — BE-44, BE-46, BE-50: both
   readings of each are laid out in
   `docs/architecture/v0.8.0-catalog-adjudication.md`; agree or rebut.
2. **The quantity identifications** — each entry of
   `QUANTITY_IDENTIFICATIONS` (`src/composition/compose.ts`) is an
   explicit physics judgment with rationale and citation. Currently the
   load-bearing one is T_H ≡ T (Landauer). Is it right, and under what
   qualifications?
3. **Spot-check a derivation** — `npx vitest run tests/composition/`
   reproduces every number in §3 from a fresh clone.

Disagreement with any rejection, identification, or grading is welcome;
the negative catalog (`src/bridges/rejected.ts`) records reasons
precisely so they can be rebutted with a citation.

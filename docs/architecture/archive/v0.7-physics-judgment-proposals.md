### My read

The standard textbook QCP scaling at the critical coupling IS
`ξ ~ T^(−1/z)`. The `−ν/z` form is what you get when you cross
hyperscaling with finite-temperature, which is exactly what
Hertz-Millis ran into above its upper critical dimension. There
are two coherent stories:

1. **"Below upper critical dim, use canonical `−1/z`"** — the
   textbook answer; what most QCP introductions teach.
2. **"At/above upper critical dim, hyperscaling fails and the
   `−ν/z` form survives via dangerously irrelevant operators"** —
   the Millis-1993 framework's specific contribution.

The current catalog's `−ν/z = −0.71` (with 3D Heisenberg ν ≈ 0.71,
z = 1) is consistent with story #2 IF the bridge is specifically
encoding the Hertz-Millis-above-d_c case. But the catalog `name`
field says "Hertz-Millis canonical scaling, 3D Heisenberg" — and
3D Heisenberg is BELOW the upper critical dim for the
ferromagnetic transition (d_c = 4 for ferromagnetic z=3; d_c = 2
for antiferromagnetic z=2). At d=3 with z=1 (Lorentz-invariant QCP)
the textbook `−1/z = −1.0` should hold.

So I lean: **the audit is RIGHT for this specific catalog encoding**
(3D Heisenberg below d_c). But the exponent value depends on `z`:

- If z=1 (Lorentz-invariant QCP): `−1/z = −1.0`, not `−0.71`.
- If z=2 (Hertz-Millis ferromagnetic dynamical critical exponent):
  `−1/z = −0.5`.

The catalog's `−0.71` ≈ `−ν/z` with `ν=0.7` (3D Heisenberg static)
and `z=1`. The fact that it's NOT a round number is the smoking
gun that the encoding used `ν/z` not `1/z`.

### Proposed action

**RECOMMENDATION: change the exponent + add an explicit citation
+ document the dispute resolution in `notes:`.**

|---|---|---|
| 1 (Lorentz-invariant) | `−1.00` | Generic 3D QCP at d=d_c |
| 2 (Hertz-Millis FM dynamical) | `−0.50` | Hertz-Millis ferromagnetic |

The catalog name says "Hertz-Millis canonical scaling" — Hertz-Millis
uses `z=2` (for ferromagnetic) or `z=3` (for antiferromagnetic).
The standard Hertz-Millis ferromagnetic 3D case has z=2; the

## Section 2 — BE-34: Boltzmann factor in Kibble-Zurek

### Evidence (from `docs/architecture/BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT.md` §4)

> Both models agree the `exp(−m c²/k_B T_reh)` Boltzmann factor is
> **not part of the canonical (kinematic) Kibble-Zurek mechanism**.
> They split, both at high confidence, on whether it is a
> *defensible extension*:
>
> - **Adam:** it can be legitimate — `n ≈ n_KZM × P(formation)`,
>   where the exponential is the probability of mustering enough
>   thermal energy (`~m c²`) to form a massive defect core when
>   `T_reh` caps the energy budget. Constructs a physical
>   justification.
> - **Eve:** it is double-counting — KZM (non-equilibrium freeze-out)
>   and Boltzmann statistics (equilibrium) are different mechanisms
>   that "contribute additively or in sequence, not
>   multiplicatively." States no peer-reviewed source presents
>   `n = n_KZ × exp(−m/T)`.

### My read

Eve's mechanism-conflict argument is correct in canonical KZM
literature. The non-equilibrium freeze-out scaling
`n ~ a^(−d) (τ_Q/τ_0)^(−ν d / (1+νz))` is derived from
critical-slowing-down arguments that don't reference Boltzmann
equilibration. Adding a multiplicative Boltzmann factor implies
the freeze-out density is then *suppressed* by a thermal factor —
which conflates two different physical regimes (the system is
out of equilibrium during the quench; the Boltzmann factor
assumes equilibrium).

Adam's "muster enough thermal energy" argument is intuitive but
not standard. The freeze-out IS the mechanism that sets the
defect density; defects ARE created, they don't need
post-freeze-out thermal activation to exist. The Boltzmann factor
would suppress defect formation, which is the opposite of what's
observed (defects ARE seen above any naive thermal-suppression
threshold).

**HOWEVER**: there exist legitimate modifications of KZM for
*massive* defects (cosmic strings, monopoles in BSM models) where
core-energy thresholds DO matter, and the Boltzmann factor can
enter as a suppression of formation rate ABOVE the kinematic
freeze-out density. Linde 1990 (cosmic strings) and earlier
GUT-monopole papers use thermal-suppression factors. So Adam's
argument has SOME basis but the multiplicative-not-additive
combination is non-standard.

### My read on resolution

The catalog's `name: 'Kibble-Zurek Mechanism in Curved Spacetime'`
suggests this is the **cosmological** KZM (defect formation during
post-inflationary reheating), where the Boltzmann factor for
massive defects is sometimes encountered in BSM-cosmology literature.
If THAT'S the intended interpretation, the Boltzmann factor is
defensible but should be documented as such — not presented as
part of the canonical KZM.

### Proposed action

**RECOMMENDATION: KEEP the Boltzmann factor but DOCUMENT the
non-canonical extension explicitly in `notes:` + downgrade the
`status:` accordingly.**

The status is currently `'speculative'` (already demoted per
prior session work — that demotion stands). The action here is:

- `notes:` append:

```
| 2026-05-23 BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT §4 resolution: Boltzmann factor `exp(−m c²/k_B T_reh)` is **NOT** part of the canonical kinematic KZM (Eve confirmed at high confidence; standard freeze-out derivation has no thermal-suppression). The catalog's inclusion is a non-canonical extension drawn from cosmological-KZM literature on massive defects (cosmic strings, GUT monopoles — Linde 1990, etc.) where core-energy thresholds during reheating CAN motivate a suppression factor. Multiplicative combination with the kinematic `n_KZ` is unconventional; additive or sequential composition may be more standard. **Status remains 'speculative' specifically to flag this combination**; the underlying kinematic KZM is canonical.
```

### Required user input

Confirm whether the intended interpretation is cosmological-KZM
with massive defects (in which case the proposed `notes:` update
is correct). If the intent is something else (e.g., applying KZM
to a non-cosmological lab system with thermal core-energy
threshold), the rationale changes.

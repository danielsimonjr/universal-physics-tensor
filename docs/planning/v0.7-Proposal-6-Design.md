# UPT v0.7+ Proposal 6 — Bridge Composition: The Research Track — Design

> **Scope:** A research track, NOT a release sprint. Defines what "bridge
> composition" means inside UPT, what a calibration set looks like, what
> success and failure look like at each phase, and how the deliverables
> are sequenced across **v0.8 (Phase A) → v0.9 (Phases B–C) → v1.0
> (Phase D)** per `docs/planning/UPT v0.70 - Proposals.md` §11.
>
> **Inspiration & precedent:** `UPT v0.70 - Proposals.md` §7
> (Proposal 6) and §11 (Sequencing). House style: `v0.6.1-Design.md`.
> Adversarial-review discipline: `v0.5.0-Design.md` §11
> (`Adam+Eve` caught F1/F2/F3 — the ricci-slot bug — before code
> landed). Physics-correctness tier: `docs/architecture/BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT.md`
> (84 reviews / two-model second opinion across the existing 42-bridge
> catalog).
>
> **Honest framing — read this first.** This is the **most
> speculative proposal in the v0.7+ block** — the only High-risk item
> (`risk: High (research)`). The Phase A deliverable is **a research
> framework + small calibration set + candid write-up of what works
> and what does not** — not a green test suite, not a public API, and
> possibly not even a runnable feature. If Phase A cannot represent
> at least one known cross-scale derivation, the right output is a
> "negative-result" write-up. That is a valid v0.8 deliverable.

## 0. Tag decision

**No tag of its own.** Proposal 6 is a multi-version research track,
not a release. Each phase ships into its host release: Phase A inside
`v0.8` alongside Proposal 5; Phases B+C inside `v0.9` alongside
Proposals 7 (workbooks) and 8 (AD); Phase D inside `v1.0` alongside
the methodological white paper.

Publishing is not gated on Phase A. If Phase A produces only a
research spec + a negative-result write-up, `v0.8` still ships its
other deliverables. The track is **explicitly decoupled** from the
release cadence so the engineer's discipline of catalog-and-compose-
and-validate is not held hostage to a physics question that may not
resolve on schedule.

---

## 1. Goal

Produce a defensible answer to the question: **"Can the 42-bridge UPT
catalog be composed?"** — where "composed" has a specific, falsifiable
meaning chosen up front (Decision #1).

The track succeeds if, at v1.0 close, at least one of:

1. **Positive outcome.** The framework reproduces ≥3 known cross-scale
   derivations (Phase B), survives ≥2 stress tests (Phase C), and
   generates ≥1 novel candidate worth physicist review (Phase D). UPT
   plausibly claims a methodological contribution.
2. **Negative outcome with diagnosis.** The framework fails on the
   calibration set in a way the design can name (e.g. "categorical
   composition cannot represent non-commuting order-of-limits"), and
   the failure is published as a research note in the v1.0 white
   paper. UPT plausibly claims a methodological *clarification* —
   pinning down what does NOT compose and why.

Both are valid deliverables. The track fails only if Phases A–D end
with **no clear yes-or-no** — a vague "more research needed" that
doesn't advance the field.

## 2. Architectural Decisions (locked)

### Decision #1 — Bridge composition means **numerical-cascade composition first, categorical composition second** (two-interpretation commitment)

The proposals document is hand-wavy about what "compose" means. This
design commits to a **specific pair of interpretations** in priority
order, and explicitly rejects two others.

| # | Interpretation | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | **Mathematical composition** (compose two bridge equations symbolically into a chained inference, e.g. substitute the output of Bridge A into the input of Bridge B as ASTs) | **OUT** for Phase A; **revisit at Phase B if needed** | Symbolic substitution across bridges is what the existing v0.4.0 AST machinery already supports for `CovariantDerivativeNode` and similar — extending it across bridge boundaries collides with the 16-node mathts-expression AST + the UPT physics AST (Proposal 3) in ways that demand AST design choices that Proposal 6 is not equipped to make alone. Out of scope here. |
| (b) | **Categorical composition** (express each bridge as a morphism `B: regime₁ → regime₂` in a category of physics theories, then compose by morphism composition — Baez & Stay 2010, Coecke & Kissinger 2017, Fong & Spivak 2019) | **IN (secondary)** — formal layer above (d), introduced in Phase A as documentation, exercised in Phase B as a typed composition operator | The category-theoretic framing is what gives composition associativity, identity laws, and a place to talk about non-commuting compositions (a 2-category, with 2-cells as obstructions to commutativity). This is the methodological hook in §7.3 of the proposals doc; the references already there (Baez/Stay, Coecke/Kissinger, Spivak, Fong/Spivak) point exactly at this layer. |
| (c) | **Logical composition** (treat bridges as implications, compose by transitive closure of premises) | **OUT** | Logical implication is the wrong primitive for physics bridges. A bridge `Quantum ⟶ Classical` is not "Quantum implies Classical"; it is "Quantum reduces to Classical in a specified limit, under specified flux constraints." Reduction-with-limits is what (d) captures, not implication. |
| (d) | **Numerical-cascade composition** (cascade the *numerical outputs* of one bridge as the *numerical inputs* of another, evaluated through the v0.5.0 `TensorEngine` path and — when Proposal 8 lands — AD'd end-to-end) | **IN (primary)** — exercised first in Phase A, tested against calibration cases in Phase B, stress-tested in Phase C | This is the *only* interpretation that lets the existing infrastructure (Float64ReferenceEngine, GL4 integrator, schwarzschild fixture, the v0.6.0 curvature layer) be used as machinery for evaluating "did A + B compose into the right thing?" Without (d), Phase B's calibration set is unfalsifiable — there is no number to compare against the literature. |

**The locked operational definition of bridge composition for v0.8+:**

> Two bridges `B_A: regime₁ → regime₂` and `B_B: regime₂ → regime₃`
> *compose* to `B_B ∘ B_A: regime₁ → regime₃` if, given a numerical
> instance of an observable in `regime₁` and the parameters of both
> bridges, evaluating `B_A` then `B_B` (cascade) produces an output in
> `regime₃` that matches the literature-known direct derivation of
> the same observable in `regime₃`, to within the tolerance the
> calibration case admits.

The categorical layer (b) is the *language* this definition is
expressed in; the cascade layer (d) is the *machinery* that produces
the numbers. Phase A documents (b) in a research spec; Phase B
exercises (d) on calibration cases; Phase C stresses both; Phase D
uses the composed framework to propose hypotheses.

### Decision #2 — Upstream-infrastructure dependencies are **hard prerequisites**

Proposal 6 is the last proposal in the v0.7+ block to enter execution
because it depends on three earlier proposals having shipped:

| Dep | Provides | Where it lands | Phase 6 use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proposal 3 (typed `Cell` discriminated union) | A typed `BridgeEntry` shape with `from: AxisCoordinate`, `to: AxisCoordinate`, `equation: AstNode` | v0.7 | Composition needs the typed `from`/`to` fields to determine whether two bridges *can* compose (the regime² in §1 must match). String-typed coordinates would not be safe enough to compose on. |
| Proposal 4 (Bridge DSL via `compileExpr`) | A `BridgeSum.compile()` that round-trips every v0.6 bridge through `parse()` + dimensional check | v0.7 | Numerical cascade (interpretation (d)) requires every participating bridge to have a compiled evaluator with a known dimensional signature. The DSL is the entry point. |
| Proposal 5 (`RegimeType` extensions) | A `defineRegime()` API with provenance | v0.8 Phase A (in parallel) | Composition needs a registry of regimes; the v0.6 four scales (`quantum`, `mesoscopic`, `classical`, `cosmological`) may not be enough — calibration cases below (Phase B) include `bbgky-hierarchy`, `wkb-semiclassical`, etc., which want their own regime tags. |

If any of these slips, Proposal 6 slips with it. **No hand-waving the
prerequisites** — Phase A explicitly verifies all three are in place
before starting.

The implication for Proposal 8 (AD): the cascade evaluator can run
without AD, but the *parameter inference* deliverable in Phase D
(§Phase D) requires AD across the composed cascade. If Proposal 8
ships in v0.9 as planned, Phase D inherits AD-through-composition for
free. If Proposal 8 slips, Phase D ships a structural composition
demo without parameter inference, and the AD-through-composition
becomes a v1.0+ follow-up.

### Decision #3 — Calibration set is fixed at design time, not iterated

The proposals doc §7.1 names three calibration cases ("BBGKY hierarchy
→ Vlasov → Boltzmann; semiclassical WKB; fluctuation-dissipation").
This design commits to a **specific** calibration set of **five** known
derivations and **two** stress-test cases, with literature anchors,
fixed BEFORE Phase A begins. The motivation is anti-overfitting: a
calibration set that grows as the framework grows is not a calibration
set, it is a self-confirmation loop. Failure to compose a literature
case is a real result; redefining the case to make composition succeed
is not. This is the same discipline that the BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT.md
two-model design used (§Honesty notes: "84/84 reviews completed; 2
needed one retry for output truncation").

| Case | Composition | Literature anchor | Why included |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | BBGKY truncation: N-particle Liouville → 1-particle Vlasov → Boltzmann (with collision integral) | Bogoliubov 1946; Born–Green 1946; Cercignani, *The Boltzmann Equation and its Applications* (1988) | Textbook three-level kinetic-theory hierarchy. The "right" composition produces the H-theorem. |
| C2 | Newtonian gravity ∘ Special relativity → GR weak-field limit | Misner–Thorne–Wheeler §17, §18; Carroll Ch. 4 | Weak-field GR is uniquely determined by composing Newtonian gravity with SR at low velocities and weak curvature. A composition framework that can't reproduce this is dead. |
| C3 | Semiclassical WKB: Schrödinger → Hamilton–Jacobi ∘ Geometric optics → Eikonal | Landau–Lifshitz QM §53; Bender & Orszag Ch. 10 | WKB is the canonical quantum→classical reduction. BE-37 (Shapiro delay) already lives near here. |
| C4 | Fluctuation–dissipation: microcanonical equilibrium ∘ linear response → Kubo formula | Kubo 1957; Callen–Welton 1951 | Cross-domain (statistical mech ↔ linear response) composition with a clean predictive output. |
| C5 | Hawking-temperature derivation: Bogoliubov transformation ∘ event-horizon thermodynamics → `T = ℏκ/(2πk_B c)` | Hawking 1975; Wald, *QFT in Curved Spacetime* | BE-42 is *already* in the catalog and was flagged in BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT.md §3 as "bridge-incoherence: canonical physics mis-filed." If composition can correctly *re-derive* Hawking from prior bridges, BE-42 gets a defensible composition path; if it can't, the audit finding is reinforced. |
| S1 | **Stress test:** order-of-limits in BCS superconductivity (`T → 0` vs. `V → 0`) | Tinkham, *Introduction to Superconductivity* §3 | Two limits that DO NOT commute. The framework must either (a) detect the non-commutativity and refuse to compose, or (b) be honest that it picked an ordering and which one. Either is a pass; silent commutation is a fail. |
| S2 | **Stress test:** non-commuting truncation in renormalization-group flow (Wilsonian RG between different fixed points) | Wilson 1971; Cardy *Scaling and Renormalization in Statistical Physics* §3 | RG flow between fixed points is path-dependent. A composition framework that treats RG flows as commuting morphisms is incorrect. |

This set is **frozen** at the start of Phase A. Adding cases mid-track
requires a written rationale in `docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-Calibration-Set-Amendments.md`
and Adam+Eve sign-off.

### Decision #4 — Adam+Eve adversarial review is *required* at every phase boundary, not just at release

The BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT.md precedent is that physics-correctness
verdicts demand a two-model adversarial pass. The composition
framework is a NEW physics-correctness tier (composing across the
catalog rather than auditing single bridges in isolation), so Adam+Eve
gates Phase A → Phase B → Phase C → Phase D. The cadence is:

- **End of Phase A (v0.8):** Adam+Eve review the research spec + the
  Phase A prototype. Specifically: does the categorical layer
  faithfully describe the numerical cascade? Does the cascade
  evaluator actually do what the spec says? Are there hidden
  assumptions that would make Phase B's calibration set unfalsifiable?
- **End of Phase B (v0.9 α):** Adam+Eve review each of the five
  calibration outcomes. Specifically: is the "literature-known direct
  derivation" claim true? Did the framework produce the right number
  for the right reason, or the right number by accident?
- **End of Phase C (v0.9 β):** Adam+Eve review the stress tests.
  Specifically: did the framework correctly *detect* non-commutativity,
  or did it silently pick an ordering?
- **End of Phase D (v1.0):** Adam+Eve review the candidate-hypothesis
  output. Specifically: are the proposed novel bridges physically
  meaningful, or are they syntactic noise?

This is the v0.5.0 F1/F2/F3 lesson — Adam+Eve caught the ricci-slot
permutation that would otherwise have shipped — applied at the
research tier.

Per `v0.6.1-Design.md` Decision #9: in the absence of `llm-gemini` /
`llm-openai` MCPs in this remote-execution environment, the Adam+Eve
pair runs as Opus 4.7 subagents (one steel-manning, one red-teaming)
until the cross-model MCP path is restored. Findings consolidate into
a per-phase `v0.7-Proposal-6-Phase{A,B,C,D}-Review-Findings.md`
companion document — same pattern as v0.4.0 and v0.5.0.

### Decision #5 — Categorical-layer formalism is **deliberately weak**

The category-theory references (Coecke/Kissinger, Spivak, Baez/Stay,
Fong/Spivak) are seductive — they would let UPT claim a high-powered
formal foundation. **This design declines that claim.** The
categorical layer (interpretation (b) in Decision #1) is
documentation-only in Phase A: `Part-VII-Composition.md` says
"regimes are objects; bridges are morphisms; composition is ∘;
non-commutativity is captured as a 2-cell" — with no implementation
that proves this category is well-defined.

Why deliberately weak: defining the category rigorously requires
specifying what counts as an object (what *is* a regime formally?),
what counts as a morphism (what equivalence on bridges?), and what
counts as composition (what identity bridge?). Each is itself a
research question. Phase A commits only to the *informal* statement;
Phase B/C calibration work tests whether it holds up under exercise.
If it does, Phase D promotes the categorical layer to formal in the
white paper. If it doesn't, the white paper documents where the
framing broke down — itself a useful methodological contribution.

### Decision #6 — A "white paper + minimal prototype + open questions" output is acceptable for Phase A

The Phase A deliverable is explicitly **not a feature**. It is:

1. A research spec: `docs/specification/Part-VII-Composition.md`
   (categorical framing + cascade operational definition + calibration
   set + acceptance criteria).
2. A minimal prototype: `src/composition/` with `compose(a, b)` +
   `evaluateCascade()` implementing interpretation (d) for **one or
   two** of C1–C5. Not all five. The intent is to prove the cascade
   plumbing works, not to validate it.
3. A candid open-questions document
   (`docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseA-Open-Questions.md`) listing
   every assumption the prototype made that the spec does not yet
   justify.

If Phase A produces only items (1) and (3), with item (2) flagged as
"could not be implemented within Phase A," that is a valid Phase A
outcome. Phase B then either implements item (2) as its first task,
or escalates to a negative-result publication.

### Decision #7 — Failure-mode framing is part of the deliverable

Per the proposals doc §10 "Honest Limitations," this is the highest-risk
proposal in the block. The design explicitly enumerates **five
failure modes** that would each terminate the track at different
phases. They are not risks to be mitigated — they are *expected*
possible outcomes. Each has a defined output:

| Failure mode | Phase where caught | Output |
|---|---|---|
| FM1 — The cascade evaluator is impossible to write because the v0.6 AST doesn't have a way to represent "the output of bridge A is the input of bridge B" | Phase A | Research note: "AST insufficiency for composition," documenting precisely which AST features are missing. Becomes a v0.7+ planning input for a future Proposal 3' / Proposal 4' iteration. |
| FM2 — The cascade evaluator runs but the literature-cited composition (C1–C5) cannot be reproduced for ≥2 of the cases | Phase B | Research note: "Composition framework cannot reproduce {list of failing cases}," with per-case diagnoses. v1.0 white paper documents this as a negative result. |
| FM3 — All five calibration cases pass, but the stress tests (S1, S2) reveal silent commutation of non-commuting limits | Phase C | Research note: "Framework correctly reproduces commuting compositions but fails to detect non-commutativity," with the specific cases. Track terminates at Phase C; v1.0 white paper documents the scope where composition works and where it does not. |
| FM4 — Calibration + stress all pass, but Phase D's hypothesis-generation produces only syntactic noise (composed bridges that don't survive physicist review) | Phase D | Research note: "Composition framework is internally consistent but does not advance the catalog." v1.0 white paper claims a methodological contribution (the framework) but no physics-content contribution. |
| FM5 — The track succeeds (positive outcome per §1.1) | v1.0 | White paper "UPT as a Compositional Framework for Cross-Regime Physics Claims." |

FM4 and FM5 are the *intended* outcomes. FM1, FM2, FM3 are the
falsifiability hooks that distinguish this from a methodologically
empty research program. **A research track that cannot fail is not
research.**

## 3. Phase architecture

| # | Phase | Host release | Theme | Deliverable type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Define what composition means | v0.8 | Research spec + minimal prototype + open questions | Documentation + ~200–400 LOC prototype (or 0 LOC + open-questions doc) |
| B | Calibrate on known cases | v0.9 (α) | Run cascade on C1–C5; document successes and failures | Per-case Markdown reports + suite of regression tests for the cases that pass |
| C | Stress-test on subtle cases | v0.9 (β) | Run cascade on S1–S2; verify the framework detects non-commutativity | Per-case stress-test reports + (if applicable) framework refinements |
| D | Hypothesis generation | v1.0 | Use the framework to propose candidate composed bridges | RFC-format hypothesis document + (if applicable) candidate bridge entries marked `confidence: highly-speculative` |

**Phase order is strictly sequential.** Phase A → B → C → D. No
parallel execution. The reason: Phase B's calibration set is the
*test* of Phase A's spec; running B before A is locked is a category
error. Same for C → D.

## 4. Phase A — v0.8 — Define what composition means

### A.1 Motivation

The proposals doc §7.1 commits to "Phase A (v0.8 prep): define what
composition means — dimensional consistency, limit-procedure ordering,
confidence propagation." This is the foundational research deliverable
of the track. Per Decision #1, "what composition means" has been
narrowed to **numerical-cascade composition (primary) + categorical
documentation (secondary)**.

### A.2 Methodology

Phase A is a **research methodology**, not an engineering task list:

1. **Literature review** of compositional approaches in physics:
   Coecke–Kissinger (string diagrams / compositional QM); Baez–Stay
   (Rosetta-Stone for physics/topology/logic/computation); Spivak
   (functorial data migration); Fong–Spivak (compositional general
   systems). Cross-check against BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT.md §3's finding
   that "the bridge concept is the framework's softest claim" — the
   review is partly a sanity check that "composition" is even the
   right thing to ask for in this catalog.

2. **Operational-definition draft.** Write the cascade definition
   (Decision #1) into `Part-VII-Composition.md` with worked examples
   drawn from C1–C5. Each example identifies (a) the regimes, (b) the
   bridges, (c) the numerical interface (what observable is passed?),
   (d) the literature reference for the direct derivation, (e) the
   tolerance the comparison admits.

3. **Prerequisites verification.** Confirm Proposal 3 (typed `Cell`),
   Proposal 4 (DSL), Proposal 5 (RegimeType) have shipped per
   Decision #2. If any is incomplete, **stop and document the gap**.

4. **Minimal prototype** (per Decision #6, optional):
   `src/composition/compose.ts` exports `compose(a, b)`;
   `src/composition/cascade-evaluator.ts` exports `evaluateCascade()`.
   One or two of C1–C5 implemented end-to-end. C2 (Newton + SR →
   weak-field GR) is the recommended first target — every constituent
   already exists in the v0.6 catalog or fixture set.

5. **Open-questions document.** One-paragraph entries for every
   assumption the prototype made that the spec does not yet justify.

6. **Adam+Eve adversarial review** at phase boundary (Decision #4).

### A.3 Evaluation rubric

Phase A succeeds if **all four** of the following hold:

- [ ] `Part-VII-Composition.md` defines composition operationally
      (a reader can take the document and answer "yes" or "no" to
      "did this composition succeed?" without consulting the design
      author).
- [ ] The five calibration cases (C1–C5) have been *categorized*: for
      each, the spec identifies the regimes, the bridges, and the
      observable being passed. This does NOT require the cases to be
      *implemented* — categorization alone is the Phase A bar.
- [ ] The open-questions doc lists ≥5 honest open assumptions.
      (Fewer than 5 = the design hasn't been honest about its limits;
      more than 20 = the spec is not yet stable enough for Phase B.)
- [ ] Adam+Eve sign off on "the spec is sufficient input for Phase B
      to run against."

Phase A fails (terminating to FM1) if:

- The spec cannot be written because the v0.6 AST genuinely lacks a
  way to represent "the output of one bridge is the input of another."
  In that case, the deliverable becomes the negative-result research
  note specified in FM1.

### A.4 Deliverables

- `docs/specification/Part-VII-Composition.md` (new, ~1500–3000 LOC of
  Markdown including worked examples).
- `docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseA-Open-Questions.md` (new).
- (Optional, per Decision #6) `src/composition/` with `compose()` and
  `evaluateCascade()` and a TDD suite for the one or two prototyped
  cases.
- `docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseA-Review-Findings.md` —
  Adam+Eve reconciliation.

### A.5 Risks

- **A-R1** — Categorical formalism overclaim. Mitigated by Decision #5.
- **A-R2** — Hidden dependency on unshipped Proposal 3/4/5. Mitigated
  by Decision #2's hard-prerequisite check (§A.2 step 3).
- **A-R3** — Prototype silently succeeds for the wrong reason
  (numerical agreement by accident). Mitigated by Adam+Eve's "right
  number for the right reason?" review prompt.

## 5. Phase B — v0.9 (α) — Calibrate on known cross-scale derivations

### B.1 Motivation

A framework that cannot reproduce textbook results is not a framework.
Phase B's question is purely empirical: **given C1–C5 as defined in
Decision #3, does the cascade evaluator produce the literature-known
output for each?**

### B.2 Methodology

For each calibration case Cᵢ (i = 1..5):

1. **Encode the constituent bridges.** Some are already in the v0.6
   catalog (BE-42 Hawking serves C5; geodesic machinery serves C2;
   BE-37 Shapiro neighbors C3). Others are authored fresh through
   Proposal 4's `BridgeSum`, with `confidence: 'established'` and
   explicit `references:` pointing at the Decision #3 anchor.
2. **Define the cascade graph.** Express the literature derivation
   as an ordered list of bridges: `B_1, …, B_n`. C1 is 3 bridges
   (Liouville → Vlasov → Boltzmann); C2 is 2 (Newton + SR → weak-field
   GR). Encode the graph using Phase A's `compose()`.
3. **Run the cascade.** `evaluateCascade(composed, inputs)` with
   `inputs` set to the regime-1 observable.
4. **Compare against literature.** The direct derivation produces a
   known expression (analytic) or number (parametric). Report
   relative error.
5. **Diagnose.** Match: one-page case-success report
   (`docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseB-C{i}.md`) with bridges,
   comparison, tolerance. No match: *failure* report with diagnosis —
   where did the cascade diverge? Which bridge produced the wrong
   intermediate? Bridge-content error (re-encode and retry) or
   framework error (terminating per FM2)?
6. **Adam+Eve adversarial review** at phase boundary. Per the
   BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT.md precedent, every calibration outcome is
   *independently* reviewed with the prompt: "did the framework
   produce the right number for the right reason, or by accident?"
   This is the v0.5.0 F3 lesson re-applied (Adam+Eve caught the
   ricci-slot bug because they asked not "does the test pass" but
   "is the index convention right").

### B.3 Evaluation rubric

Phase B succeeds if **at least 3 of 5** calibration cases reproduce
the literature outcome (per-case tolerance documented in the case's
worked example in `Part-VII-Composition.md`).

Phase B partially succeeds if **2 of 5** cases reproduce, with the
failures diagnosed as bridge-content errors (re-encoding work, then
re-attempt). Partial success becomes full success in a Phase B'
follow-up if the re-encoded bridges then pass.

Phase B fails (terminating to FM2) if **0 or 1** of 5 cases reproduce,
or if the failures cannot be diagnosed. In that case the v1.0 white
paper documents the framework as not having achieved its goal.

### B.4 Deliverables

- `docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseB-C{1..5}.md` — per-case
  reports (success or failure, with full diagnosis).
- `tests/composition/calibration/` — regression suite for the cases
  that passed. The suite asserts the *exact* numerical outputs that
  Phase B locked in, so future framework changes can't silently
  invalidate the calibration.
- `docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseB-Review-Findings.md` — Adam+Eve.

### B.5 Risks

- **B-R1** — v0.6 catalog constituents (e.g. BE-42 for C5) are
  flagged in BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT.md §3 as framing-overreach /
  bridge-incoherence. A dubious constituent contaminates the cascade.
  Mitigated by **per-constituent audit**: before running a case,
  verify each constituent bridge passes `dimensional-signature-catalog.test.ts`
  AND has a clean audit entry (or has been re-encoded since).
- **B-R2** — Tolerance gerrymandering. Mitigated by Decision #3: the
  per-case tolerance is fixed *before* the cascade is run.
- **B-R3** — A case "passes" because two errors cancelled. Mitigated
  by Adam+Eve's "right number for the right reason?" review.

## 6. Phase C — v0.9 (β) — Stress-test on subtle cases

### C.1 Motivation

A framework that reproduces textbook results may still silently mis-handle
the cases where the textbook is itself careful — order-of-limits,
non-commuting truncations, path-dependence. Phase C tests S1 (BCS
T→0 vs V→0) and S2 (RG flow between fixed points) per Decision #3.

### C.2 Methodology

For each stress case Sᵢ (i = 1..2):

1. **Define both orderings.** S1 has two: `lim_{V→0} lim_{T→0}` vs
   `lim_{T→0} lim_{V→0}`. Encode both as cascade graphs.
2. **Run both.** Capture both outputs. They MUST differ (the physics
   fact under test).
3. **Verify the framework's response.** Acceptable behaviors:
   - **(a) Detect** — raise `NonCommutingCompositionWarning` and
     refuse a default ordering without explicit user choice.
   - **(b) Disclose** — pick one ordering (e.g. authored order) and
     emit a clearly visible note that the other would differ.
   - **(c) Refuse** — return a `CompositionError` of kind
     `'non-commuting-limits'` and ask the caller to pick.

   Unacceptable: silently use one ordering with no indication. FM3
   catches this.
4. **Document.** Per-case report
   (`docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseC-S{i}.md`) with both
   outputs, the framework's response, and an (a)/(b)/(c)/fail
   assessment.
5. **Adam+Eve adversarial review**: "is the framework's response to
   non-commutativity defensible, or is it papering over a problem?"

### C.3 Evaluation rubric

Phase C succeeds if both S1 and S2 are handled by **one of (a), (b),
or (c)** — not by silent ordering. Different cases can have different
responses; what matters is that the framework never silently chooses.

Phase C fails (terminating to FM3) if either stress case is handled
by silent ordering, or if the framework crashes / returns nonsense on
either case.

### C.4 Deliverables

- `docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseC-S{1,2}.md` — per-case reports.
- `tests/composition/stress/` — regression suite that locks the
  framework's response. (E.g., for S1 the test asserts that *either*
  a warning is emitted OR an error is thrown; silent success fails
  the test.)
- `docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseC-Review-Findings.md` — Adam+Eve.

### C.5 Risks

- **C-R1** — The non-commutativity detection is itself a heuristic
  (re-run with swapped order, check inequality). Heuristics can have
  false negatives. Mitigated by documenting the mechanism in the case
  report; if heuristic, the report says so.
- **C-R2** — S1 or S2's literature treatment is itself contested.
  Mitigated by Decision #3's literature anchors (Tinkham §3, Wilson
  1971 / Cardy §3); if the anchor is wrong, reformulate by amendment,
  not by silent redefinition.

## 7. Phase D — v1.0 — Hypothesis generation

### D.1 Motivation

The proposals doc §7.1 frames Phase D as "use the engine to *propose*
candidate bridges as hypotheses for physicist review." This is the
methodologically most ambitious phase: instead of validating known
compositions, the framework is asked to *suggest* new ones.

### D.2 Methodology

1. **Pair-enumerate.** From the 42-bridge catalog, enumerate all
   ordered pairs `(B_A, B_B)` where `B_A.to == B_B.from` (Proposal 2's
   `unpopulatedNeighborhoods()` is this query at different
   granularity). Expected count: low hundreds at the 42-bridge scale.
2. **Filter by composability.** The intermediate observable from
   `B_A` must typecheck as an input to `B_B`. Proposal 3's typed
   `Cell` makes this a TypeScript-level check.
3. **Score by novelty.** Compositions matching an existing catalog
   bridge (e.g. BE-52 ≈ C2 = Newton + SR weak-field) are skipped.
   The rest are *candidate novel bridges*.
4. **Score by physical plausibility.** This requires physicist
   judgment, not algorithmic scoring. The framework's role is to
   *surface* candidates ranked by a defensible metric (shortest
   cascade chain, regime distance), then a physicist reviews. Per
   proposals doc §10.4, this is the social bottleneck the research
   track partly aims to lower.
5. **RFC publication.** Top-K candidates (K ≈ 5–10) become a public
   RFC: "Candidate Composed Bridges from the UPT Composition
   Framework, v1.0," inviting working-physicist commentary.
6. **Adam+Eve adversarial review** of each candidate: "is this
   physically meaningful, or syntactic noise that happens to
   typecheck?"

### D.3 Evaluation rubric

Phase D succeeds if **at least 1 candidate** survives Adam+Eve review
and is published in the RFC as worth physicist commentary. The bar is
deliberately low; Phase D is not asked to *prove* a candidate, only
to *propose* a candidate that experts find worth their time.

Phase D fails (terminating to FM4) if **all candidates** are flagged
by Adam+Eve as syntactic noise. In that case the v1.0 white paper
makes the negative-but-still-useful claim that the framework is
internally consistent but does not advance the catalog.

### D.4 Deliverables

- `docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseD-RFC.md` — the candidate-bridge
  RFC.
- Candidate `BridgeEntry`s as `confidence: 'highly-speculative'`
  entries in the catalog, clearly marked `provenance: 'composition-generated'`.
  These are NOT promoted to the catalog's "established" or
  "speculative" tiers without physicist sign-off — they live in a
  segregated namespace.
- `docs/specification/Part-VII-Composition.md` final revision —
  promoted from informal categorical framing to whatever level of
  formalism Phases B and C have justified.
- The v1.0 white paper "UPT as a Compositional Framework for
  Cross-Regime Physics Claims" (or its negative-result analogue per
  FM5/FM4).
- `docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-PhaseD-Review-Findings.md` —
  Adam+Eve.

### D.5 Risks

- **D-R1** — RFC silence. If no physicist responds, the social-barrier
  problem (proposals doc §10.4) dominates. Partially mitigated by
  parallel outreach (sympathetic research group as anchor); not
  solved by this design.
- **D-R2** — A candidate looks novel but is a known result the author
  was unaware of. Mitigated by mandatory pre-RFC literature search +
  Adam+Eve's "is this already known?" prompt.
- **D-R3** — Candidates are dimensionally consistent but physically
  meaningless (typechecks, not physics). Per Decision #5 this is the
  *expected* FM4 outcome — internally consistent framework, no
  physics-content contribution.

## 8. Adam+Eve adversarial review plan (cross-phase)

Per Decision #4, the review pair runs at each phase boundary, not
just at v1.0. Each phase ships a Review-Findings document in the
`docs/planning/v0.7-Proposal-6-Phase{A,B,C,D}-Review-Findings.md`
series.

The standing per-phase prompts:

- **Phase A:** "Does the spec define composition operationally?
  Are there hidden assumptions that would make Phase B unfalsifiable?"
- **Phase B:** "Did the framework produce the right number for the
  right reason, or the right number by accident? Is the per-case
  tolerance justified or gerrymandered?"
- **Phase C:** "Is the framework's response to non-commutativity
  defensible, or is it papering over a problem?"
- **Phase D:** "Are the proposed novel bridges physically meaningful,
  or are they syntactic noise that happens to typecheck?"

Per `v0.6.1-Design.md` Decision #9 and v0.5.1 carry-forward: cross-model
MCP (Gemini + o3) is the preferred substrate for these prompts because
the BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT.md precedent established that two distinct
frontier reasoning models produce useful disagreement (14 of 42 cases
diverged — that's the design working). If the cross-model MCPs are
unavailable, Opus 4.7 subagents are the fallback. Every Adam+Eve
finding requires grep-verification before action — per v0.5.1's
lesson, 5 of 9 Eve-vet challenges in v0.5.1 were fabrications, and
the same hygiene applies to this research tier.

## 10. Non-goals

Explicitly NOT in scope for Proposal 6 (any phase):

- **Mathematical (symbolic) composition** — interpretation (a) in
  Decision #1; deferred to a future proposal.
- **Logical composition** — interpretation (c); rejected entirely.
- **A categorical-foundations PhD thesis.** Decision #5 keeps the
  categorical layer documentation-only.
- **Replacing any existing bridge** in the v0.6 catalog with a
  composed version. Phase D adds candidates to a segregated
  namespace; the existing 42 are untouched.
- **Promoting composition-generated candidates** to
  `confidence: 'established'` or `'speculative'` without physicist
  sign-off. They live as `'highly-speculative'` with
  `provenance: 'composition-generated'`.
- **Solving the social-barrier problem** (proposals doc §10.4).
  Phase D's RFC is a pre-requisite to outreach, not outreach itself.
- **Optimizing cascade-evaluator performance.** The question is *can
  it compose*, not *how fast*. Calibration cases that take minutes
  per run are acceptable.
- **Publishing `src/composition/`** to npm. Per Decision #6 it can
  ship as `@internal` and unreleased; the deliverable is the
  research, not the package.

## 12. Honest closing note

The proposals doc positions this as "where UPT could contribute
something genuinely new methodologically." The design accepts that
framing — with the caveats in Decisions #5, #6, #7. **The
contribution may be the framework, or it may be the candid published
account of why the framework didn't work.** Both are useful. Neither
is guaranteed.

The single most important commitment is Decision #3's frozen
calibration set + Decision #7's failure-mode framing. Together they
make the track *falsifiable*. A research track that cannot produce a
"no" is not research. If C1–C5 don't compose, that is information,
and the right output is the negative-result white paper — not a
redefined calibration set.

The single most important external dependency is Proposal 3 (typed
`Cell`) shipping cleanly in v0.7. Without typed `from`/`to` on
`BridgeEntry`, the Phase D cascade typecheck cannot run at compile
time and the framework loses its strongest correctness guarantee.

Per the v0.5.0 lesson: never assume the plan inherits its design's
fixes. The upcoming Phase A implementation plan (start of v0.8) will
be vetted independently. The plan's Adam+Eve vet must grep-confirm
that every §2 decision is reproduced in the task list, and that
Decision #3's calibration set is exactly what the plan proposes to
exercise — not a "lightly updated" version.
